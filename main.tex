\begin{abstract}
Long-lived embodied agents must navigate worlds that continue to evolve while
unobserved. Their memories can remain faithful to past observations yet provide
obsolete guidance for present actions. We formulate \emph{persistent navigation
in evolving worlds}: inferring the current task-relevant state from intermittent
experience and gathering physical evidence to resolve the resulting uncertainty.
We introduce \textsc{EvolvingWorldNav}, an embodied agent that converts 4D
memory---persistent 3D entities with temporal state histories---into a belief
over the present. A continuous-time Transformer encodes irregular observations,
and a persistence--relocation head separates whether the last observed state
remains valid from where it may have transitioned. The full belief guides
cost-aware search; visibility-aware Bayesian updates distinguish an informative
negative observation from an inconclusive inspection and trigger replanning.
This loop operates with a frozen vision--language controller. To evaluate hidden
world evolution, we introduce \pfdbench{}, a human-trace-grounded benchmark with
\textbf{54} scenes and \textbf{803.68k} tasks spanning state prediction,
navigation, and embodied question answering. On P4D-Bench, our agent achieves
\textbf{61.32\%} first-attempt success, improving over the strongest baseline by
\textbf{15.99} percentage points, and reaches \textbf{86.18\%} search success
with \textbf{70.15\%} SPL. It also achieves \textbf{63.22\%} HL-SR on FindingDory
and \textbf{35.43\%} SR on GOAT-Bench. Component ablations and paired controller
comparisons support the contribution of predictive memory across VLMs, while
paired static, routine, and random worlds show that gains reflect learnable
temporal evolution rather than scene-frequency shortcuts. These results
establish predictive memory as a basis for navigation when past observations
no longer specify the present world.
\end{abstract}
