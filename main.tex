\begin{abstract}
Embodied navigation is typically formulated assuming that maps and memories describe a stationary world. This assumption breaks for long-lived agents, where environments evolve during periods of absence and historical observations can become correct yet decisionally stale. We formulate \emph{persistent navigation in evolving worlds}, where an agent must infer the current task-relevant state from incomplete temporal experience, act under uncertainty, and update its beliefs through physical evidence. We introduce \evolvingworldnav{}, a predictive embodied-agent framework centered on \pfdnav{}, which transforms evidence-grounded 4D memory into an actionable belief over the present world. A continuous-time temporal model captures irregular histories, while a persistence--relocation formulation predicts whether previous states remain valid or where objects are likely to transition. The resulting belief guides cost-aware navigation and is refined through visibility-aware Bayesian updates during interaction. We further introduce \pfdbench{}, a \textbf{54}-scene benchmark with \textbf{803.68k} tasks spanning state prediction, object navigation, vision-and-language navigation, and embodied question answering. Across diverse benchmarks, EvolvingWorldNav achieves \textbf{63.22\%} HL-SR and \textbf{48.83\%} HL-SPL on FindingDory, \textbf{35.43\%} SR on GOAT-Bench, and \textbf{86.18\%} Search SR with \textbf{70.15\%} SPL on P4D-Bench. Controlled static, routine, and random worlds further show that the agent learns temporal evolution rather than exploiting scene-frequency shortcuts. Our work shifts embodied navigation from acting on remembered past observations toward maintaining, testing, and acting on beliefs about the world as it is now.
\end{abstract}
