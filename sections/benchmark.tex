\section{EvolvingWorld-Bench: Evaluating Navigation in Evolving Worlds}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\evolvingworldbench{} evaluates decisions based on time-ordered observation
histories while unobserved changes occur in the world. Across 54 scenes,
803.68k task instances combine persistent histories, executable placements,
and controlled dynamics.

\subsection{Tasks and Controlled Dynamics}

\Needspace{0.20\textheight}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.58\textwidth}
    \centering
    \vspace{-8pt}
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/P4D_Benchmark.pdf}
    \caption{Overview of \evolvingworldbench{} evaluation protocols.}
    \label{fig:p4dbench}
\end{wrapfigure}

Grouped by task modality (\cref{fig:evolvingworldnav_overview}), the task pool
comprises current-state prediction (17\%), object navigation (27\%),
vision-and-language navigation (VLN; 26\%), and embodied question answering
(EQA; 30\%) \citep{majumdar2024openeqa,sakamoto2024map}. The protocol exports
in \cref{fig:p4dbench} provide a complementary breakdown: N1 (13.51\%), N2
(13.37\%), N3 (13.27\%), N4 (26.52\%), N5 (3.34\%), and EQA (29.99\%). In this
breakdown, VLN-conditioned episodes are assigned to the corresponding
navigation protocol rather than counted separately. The prediction task
evaluates a calibrated belief over regions or receptacles independently of
control; navigation uses the same target--time queries
(\cref{fig:p4dbench,tab:tasks}). N1 inspects the Top-1
destination; N2 uses the full belief for cost-aware search; N3 adds
visibility-qualified updates and recovery. N1--N3 fix the target after the
query. N4 advances the world clock during execution, permitting further hidden
transitions. N5 applies these protocols to held-out scenes and households.

VLN grounds language goals in the same histories; EQA probes current and
historical states without future evidence. Our experiments emphasize prediction
and navigation, with EQA used to assess memory. Controlled comparisons use
matched public observations, candidate sets, geometry, initial poses,
controllers, and budgets.

Paired static, routine, and random worlds share public setup variables. Static
worlds preserve the last supported state, and routine worlds follow household
dynamics. Random worlds match legal candidates and marginal movement statistics
while removing temporal dependencies. Improvements specific to routine worlds
thus support reliance on historical regularity rather than category frequency
or spatial convenience.

\subsection{World Construction and Past-Only Evaluation}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.50\textwidth}
    \centering
    \vspace{-6pt}
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/dataset-generate.pdf}
    \caption{Construction of \evolvingworldbench{}.}
    \label{fig:p4dbench-generation}
\end{wrapfigure}

Human traces constrain generation; source trajectories are not copied
(\cref{fig:p4dbench-generation}). CASAS supplies longitudinal occupancy and
timing statistics; ARAS and OPPORTUNITY add activity-context coverage; and
HD-EPIC and ParaHome provide object--action and motion evidence
\citep{cook2013casas,perrett2025hdepic,kim2025parahome}. HOMER+ is tracked
separately as simulated long-horizon routine evidence \citep{patel2023routine}.
Event records preserve source identifiers and distinguish measured,
environment-derived, simulated, and benchmark-authored quantities
(\appref{app:benchmark-construction}).

Chronological histories across 54 HSSD scenes \citep{khanna2024hssd} are
generated from household habits and stochastic exceptions. The stable, routine,
personal, and irregular regimes test persistence, activity regularity,
owner-specific habits, and uncertainty (\cref{tab:mobility-regimes}); regime
labels remain hidden from the agent. Habitat replay ensures semantically valid,
collision-free placements and reachable inspection viewpoints.

Patrols collect visibility-qualified RGB-D observations using only maps, time,
travel cost, and observed history. Queries expose only observations available
by the query time, the last supported state, elapsed and calendar time,
candidates, and spatial context; current states, transitions, activities,
mobility labels, and oracle viewpoints remain private. Complete temporal
sequences, paired worlds, and groups of near-duplicate queries and transitions
are kept together within data splits. Audits assess temporal and metadata
leakage, split overlap, paired-world consistency, physical validity, and patrol
access to hidden state.
Stratification covers mobility, transition, staleness, spatial difficulty, and
generalization.
