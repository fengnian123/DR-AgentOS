\section{P4D-Bench: Evaluating Navigation in Evolving Worlds}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\pfdbench{} tests decisions from causal histories while the world changes
outside observation. Its 54 scenes and 803.68k task instances combine
persistent histories, executable placements, and paired dynamics controls.

\subsection{World Construction and Causal Observation}

Human traces constrain generation without being copied as simulated trajectories.
CASAS \citep{cook2013casas}, ARAS, and HOMER+ \citep{patel2023routine} inform
schedules and activity patterns; HD-EPIC, ParaHome
\citep{perrett2025hdepic,kim2025parahome}, and OPPORTUNITY provide interaction
and placement evidence. A common event schema preserves provenance and marks
unsupported quantities as benchmark-authored (\cref{fig:p4dbench-generation}).

Across 54 HSSD scenes \citep{khanna2024hssd}, fixed household habits guide
chronological activity and object-transition generation, with stochastic
exceptions. Stable, routine, personal, and irregular mobility regimes test
persistence, activity regularity, owner-specific habits, and uncertainty
(\cref{tab:mobility-regimes}). Paired \emph{static}, \emph{routine}, and
\emph{random} worlds share public setup variables: static preserves the last
supported state, routine follows household dynamics, and random matches legal
candidates and marginal movement statistics while removing temporal dependencies.

Habitat replay enforces semantically valid, collision-free placements with
reachable inspection viewpoints. Patrol decisions use only maps, time, costs,
and observed history; local RGB-D snapshots supply visibility-qualified positive
and negative evidence. Public query inputs contain pre-query observations, the
last supported state, elapsed and calendar time, candidates, and spatial context.
Current states, hidden transitions and activities, mobility labels, and oracle
viewpoints remain private. These controls separate historical inference from
privileged access to the evolving world.

\subsection{Tasks, Protocols, and Evaluation}

The task pool comprises current-state prediction (16\%), object navigation
(27\%), vision-and-language navigation (VLN; 29\%), and embodied question
answering (EQA; 28\%) \citep{majumdar2024openeqa,sakamoto2024map}.
Prediction and navigation share target--time queries
(\cref{fig:p4dbench,tab:tasks}). N1 inspects the Top-1 destination; N2 uses the
full belief for cost-aware search; N3 adds visibility-aware updates and recovery.
These protocols fix the target after the query. N4 permits further hidden
transitions during execution; N5 applies the protocols to held-out scenes and
households.

VLN grounds language goals in the same histories; EQA probes current and
historical states without future evidence. Our experiments emphasize prediction
and navigation, with EQA as a memory diagnostic. Controlled comparisons match
observations, candidates, geometry, starts, controllers, and budgets.

Splits keep temporal sequences, paired worlds, and near-duplicate query or
transition groups intact. Audits check future evidence, private metadata,
split overlap, paired-world consistency, physical validity, and patrol access
to hidden state. Results are stratified by mobility, transition class,
staleness, spatial difficulty, and generalization split; construction details,
validity rules, and metrics appear in \cref{app:benchmark-protocol}.
