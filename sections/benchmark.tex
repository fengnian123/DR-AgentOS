\section{P4D-Bench: Evaluating Navigation in Evolving Worlds}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\pfdbench{} evaluates decisions from causal observation histories while the
world changes outside observation. Its 54 scenes and 803.68k task instances
combine persistent histories, executable placements, and controlled dynamics.

\subsection{Tasks and Controlled Dynamics}

\FloatBarrier
\Needspace{0.34\textheight}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.58\textwidth}
    \centering
    \vspace{-8pt}
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/P4D_Benchmark.pdf}
    \caption{Overview of \pfdbench{} evaluation protocols.}
    \label{fig:p4dbench}
\end{wrapfigure}

The task pool comprises current-state prediction (16\%), object navigation
(27\%), vision-and-language navigation (VLN; 29\%), and embodied question
answering (EQA; 28\%) \citep{majumdar2024openeqa,sakamoto2024map}.
Prediction evaluates a calibrated region/receptacle belief independently of
control; navigation uses the same target--time queries
(\cref{fig:p4dbench,tab:tasks}). N1 inspects the Top-1 destination; N2 uses the
full belief for cost-aware search; N3 adds visibility-qualified updates and
recovery. N1--N3 fix the target after the query. N4 advances the world clock
during execution, permitting further hidden transitions. N5 applies these protocols to held-out scenes and households.

VLN grounds language goals in the same histories; EQA probes current and
historical states without future evidence. Our experiments emphasize prediction
and navigation, with EQA as a memory diagnostic. Controlled comparisons match public
observations, candidates, geometry, starts, controllers, and budgets.

Paired \emph{static}, \emph{routine}, and \emph{random} worlds share public setup
variables. Static preserves the last supported state; routine follows household
dynamics; random matches legal candidates and marginal movement statistics while
removing temporal dependencies. Improvements specific to routine worlds thus
support the use of historical regularity over category frequency or spatial
convenience.

\subsection{World Construction and Causal Evaluation}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.58\textwidth}
    \centering
    \vspace{-8pt}
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/dataset-generate.pdf}
    \caption{Human-trace-grounded construction of \pfdbench{}.}
    \label{fig:p4dbench-generation}
\end{wrapfigure}

Human traces constrain generation rather than supply copied trajectories
(\cref{fig:p4dbench-generation}). CASAS \citep{cook2013casas}, ARAS, and HOMER+
\citep{patel2023routine} inform schedules and activities; HD-EPIC, ParaHome
\citep{perrett2025hdepic,kim2025parahome}, and OPPORTUNITY supply interaction
and placement evidence. A shared event schema preserves provenance and marks
benchmark-authored assumptions.

Across 54 HSSD scenes \citep{khanna2024hssd}, fixed household habits and
stochastic exceptions generate chronological object histories. Stable, routine,
personal, and irregular regimes test persistence, activity regularity,
owner-specific habits, and uncertainty (\cref{tab:mobility-regimes}); labels
are hidden. Habitat replay enforces semantically valid, collision-free placements
with reachable inspection viewpoints.

Patrols use only maps, time, travel cost, and observed history, producing
visibility-qualified RGB-D evidence. Queries expose causal observations, the
last supported state, elapsed and calendar time, candidates, and spatial context.
Current states, hidden transitions and activities, mobility labels, and oracle
viewpoints remain private.

Splits keep temporal sequences, paired worlds, and near-duplicate query or
transition groups intact. Audits check temporal and metadata leakage, split
overlap, paired-world consistency, physical validity, and patrol access to
hidden state. Results are stratified by mobility, transition, staleness,
spatial difficulty, and generalization; details appear in
\cref{app:benchmark-protocol}.
