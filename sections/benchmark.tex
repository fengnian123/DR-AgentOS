\section{P4D-Bench: Evaluating Navigation in Evolving Worlds}
\label{sec:benchmark}

Existing navigation benchmarks typically expose a fixed scene or reset the
environment between episodes, making the agent's latest map a valid account of
the world. They therefore cannot isolate the central difficulty of our setting:
the world may change during an observation gap, while only the agent's causal
history is available at decision time. We introduce \pfdbench{} as a dedicated
benchmark for this gap. The full release contains 54 scenes and 803.68k task
instances over longitudinal hidden state changes. It couples continuous
world evolution with executable embodied episodes so that the same latent state
can be evaluated through prediction, navigation, language-conditioned behavior,
and embodied question answering.

\subsection{Design Principles and Scope}

\pfdbench{} is designed around four principles. First, \emph{temporal
continuity}: examples are sampled from persistent scene histories rather than
independent object placements. Second, \emph{causal observability}: the public
memory contains only evidence that the robot could have acquired before the
query. Third, \emph{physical executability}: predicted states correspond to
collision-free placements with reachable, visibility-checked inspection
viewpoints. Fourth, \emph{controlled dynamics}: paired worlds keep the scene,
target, query, and marginal placement factors fixed while varying the hidden
transition mechanism. These controls separate predictive reasoning from scene
frequency, route geometry, and privileged simulator access.

The task pool comprises current-state prediction (16\%), object navigation
(27\%), vision-and-language navigation (29\%), and embodied question answering
(28\%). Prediction probes the belief independently of control. Navigation tests
whether that belief produces useful destinations, search orders, and recovery.
VLN supplies language-conditioned goals over the same evolving histories, while
EQA probes whether memory preserves the evidence needed to reason about current
and historical states. The controlled experiments in this paper emphasize state
prediction and navigation, with EQA as a memory diagnostic; the remaining task
annotations are included to support broader study of evolving-world agents.

\subsection{Human-Trace-Grounded World Evolution}

\paragraph{Source alignment.}
We derive complementary statistics from longitudinal smart-home records such as
CASAS \citep{cook2013casas} and ARAS, and action--object evidence from
EPIC-KITCHENS-100 and OPPORTUNITY. These sources are normalized into a common
event schema linking time, activity, actor context, object category, source
state, destination state, and evidence provenance. They constrain schedules,
activity--object associations, transition timing, and placement tendencies; no
source trajectory is copied verbatim into simulation. Unsupported quantities
are explicitly marked as benchmark-authored rather than presented as measured
human behavior.

\paragraph{Persistent household generation.}
Across 54 HSSD scenes \citep{khanna2024hssd} and multi-day household histories, we
freeze household-level factors including resident schedules, room preferences,
orderliness, object ownership, and placement habits. We then jointly generate
activities, resident region trajectories, and object transitions in chronological
order. Frozen factors make earlier observations informative about later states,
whereas stochastic events, delayed returns, and exceptions prevent the histories
from becoming deterministic scripts. The audited configuration yields 42.6
region transitions per resident-day, 6.56 relocations per personal object-day,
52.6\% within-region relocations, and zero terminal collisions.

\subsection{Controlled Dynamics and Mobility Regimes}

Every relocation is attributable to an activity, resident transition, object
lifecycle event, or tidying event. Targets follow one of four complementary
mechanisms shown in \cref{tab:mobility-regimes}. Stable or rarely moved objects
measure whether a predictive method preserves valid history. Routine objects
follow repeated activity chains. Personal objects alternate between placed and
carried states and exhibit owner-specific return habits. Irregular objects use
affordance-valid destinations without temporal or actor-specific predictability.
Mobility labels support stratified analysis but are hidden from the agent.

For each compatible history and query, we construct paired \emph{static},
\emph{routine}, and \emph{random} worlds. Static preserves the last supported
state; routine samples from the household's frozen transition process; and random
matches legal candidates and marginal movement statistics while breaking the
learnable temporal dependency. Pair members share all public setup variables.
Consequently, improvement specific to routine worlds is evidence that a model
uses historical regularity rather than category frequency or spatial convenience.
Audits additionally verify that personal objects do not simply track their owners
and that actor or activity identity does not predict irregular destinations beyond
affordance.

\subsection{Physical Realization and Causal Observation}

Generated transitions are replayed chronologically in Habitat. Only semantically
valid, collision-free placements with at least one reachable inspection viewpoint
enter the benchmark. For every candidate, we record navigability, geodesic cost,
camera frustum coverage, unoccluded target fraction, and success viewpoints.
These checks prevent a method from being penalized for an impossible placement
or an unreachable observation.

A robot patrols using the map, time, travel cost, and its own accumulated history;
it never observes current object states, resident locations, hidden activities,
or generator latents. Region visits produce local RGB-D snapshots that are
converted into positive and negative observations with visibility metadata. For
a target queried at $t_q$, the public input terminates at or before $t_q$ and
contains the observed history, last supported state, elapsed time, calendar
context, legal candidates, and spatial context. The evaluator retains the current
state, hidden transitions, query-time activity, mobility regime, and oracle
viewpoints. Query times cover both the natural temporal distribution and
mechanism-relevant windows, such as stages of a routine or the interval after a
personal object is dropped.

\subsection{Tasks and Evaluation Protocols}

\pfdbench{} derives all prediction and navigation episodes from the same
target--time queries (\cref{fig:p4dbench,tab:tasks}). The prediction track asks
for a calibrated belief over the current region or receptacle without navigation
confounds. A navigation episode augments that query with a start pose, candidate
inspection viewpoints, a path budget, and frozen success conditions. N1 permits
only the Top-1 predicted destination and measures whether prediction becomes a
useful physical decision. N2 exposes the full belief and measures cost-aware
search. N3 updates the belief after visibility-qualified observations and tests
recovery from an incorrect first destination. N4 advances the world clock during
execution and can introduce another hidden transition. N5 is not a separate task;
it applies the preceding protocols to held-out scenes and households.

The EQA suite queries existence, location, temporal order, activity association,
count, comparison, and compositional relations over the same causal memories.
Answers are evaluated against a normalized ontology, and questions requiring
future evidence are excluded. VLN episodes replace an object label with a
language-conditioned goal whose referents and constraints are grounded in the
same versioned scene history. Across tracks, methods receive identical public
observations, candidates, geometry, starts, controllers, and budgets; only their
memory representation, current-state belief, and resulting decisions differ.

\subsection{Splits, Leakage Prevention, and Quality Control}

We split complete temporal sequences rather than individual queries so that no
future portion of a household day can leak into an earlier decision. Held-out
homes are reserved for cross-scene evaluation, and paired static/routine/random
members remain in the same split. Near-duplicate queries and transition groups
are assigned atomically. The serialized release is audited for future timestamps,
private event fields, answer-correlated filenames or indices, repeated event
groups across splits, inconsistent paired variants, invalid physics, unreachable
goals, and accidental dependence of the patrol policy on hidden state.

Results are reported by mobility regime, transition class, staleness, spatial
difficulty, and generalization split. We also expose dataset cards and audit
summaries for source mappings, authored assumptions, physical validity, candidate
coverage, and leakage tests. Full schema definitions, episode validity rules, and
metric implementations are provided in the supplement.
