\section{Limitations}

\evolvingworldnav{} is motivated by general task-relevant world evolution, but
the current predictive model primarily represents discrete states of portable
objects. Structural reconfiguration and changing traversability require richer
state and action models. \pfdnav{} also assumes reliable entity association
and an available set of candidate receptacles and inspection viewpoints. Pose
drift, missed detections, identity switches, or missing candidates can corrupt
both prediction and navigation. The discrete belief represents arbitrary
continuous placements less directly than continuous generative models. The
source datasets contain complementary observations collected independently.
Episodes in \evolvingworldbench{} are therefore simulations grounded in human
traces rather than reconstructions of a single household. N1--N3 isolate
query-time inference by fixing the target during navigation, while N4 evaluates
further hidden transitions during execution. Broader interacting-object
dynamics remain future work. Visibility-aware updates reduce the influence of
weak negative evidence but cannot guarantee recovery when beliefs and
perception fail together or routines shift.

The real-world evaluation covers indoor and outdoor environments and three
physical robot platforms, but does not represent unrestricted long-term
deployment. Targets may change between observation sessions but remain
stationary during each individual search episode. Continuously moving targets
would additionally require online tracking and motion prediction. All three
evaluated platforms are from the same manufacturer, and the current
study does not establish transfer to substantially different sensing,
localization, or control stacks. Real-world performance still depends
on the underlying perception and navigation systems: localization errors,
missed detections, and navigation failures can prevent task completion even
when the high-level belief is correct.
