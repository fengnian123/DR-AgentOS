\section{Conclusion}

We study persistent navigation under unobserved changes before and during
execution. \evolvingworldnav{} combines a persistence--relocation belief
derived from 4D histories with an event-driven predict--observe--replan filter.
Evaluations on \evolvingworldbench{}, external benchmarks, and physical robots
show improvements in first-inspection selection and recovery, especially when
changes exhibit learnable temporal structure. Future work will address
interacting objects and continuously changing goals. More broadly, the results
suggest that persistent embodied memory should be evaluated by its ability to
support calibrated inference and evidence-seeking action as the world changes,
as well as by the historical information it stores.
