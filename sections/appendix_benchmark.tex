\subsection{Extended Benchmark Design and Construction}
\label{app:benchmark-extended-design}

\paragraph{Design principles.}
Temporal continuity samples examples from persistent scene histories rather than
independent object placements. Causal observability restricts public memory to
evidence available to the robot before the query. Physical executability requires
collision-free placements with reachable, visibility-checked inspection
viewpoints. Controlled dynamics hold scene, target, query, and marginal placement
factors fixed while varying the hidden transition mechanism. Together, these
controls separate predictive reasoning from scene frequency, route geometry,
and privileged simulator access.

\subsubsection{Source Alignment and Household Generation}
\label{app:world-generation}

Longitudinal smart-home records from CASAS \citep{cook2013casas} and ARAS provide
complementary schedule statistics; HOMER+ \citep{patel2023routine} provides
multi-week household activity and object-location sequences. HD-EPIC
\citep{perrett2025hdepic} supplies fine-grained egocentric action traces,
ParaHome \citep{kim2025parahome} provides reconstructed human--object
interactions, and HOMER+ captures longer-term variation in routine object use.
OPPORTUNITY supplies additional evidence. Their common schema links time,
activity, actor context, object category, source state, destination state, and
evidence provenance. Source mappings and unsupported benchmark-authored
assumptions are exposed in dataset cards and audit summaries.

Across multi-day histories in 54 HSSD scenes \citep{khanna2024hssd}, frozen
household factors include resident schedules, room preferences, orderliness,
object ownership, and placement habits. Activities, resident region trajectories,
and object transitions are jointly generated in chronological order. Stochastic
events, delayed returns, and exceptions prevent deterministic scripts. Household
profiles, transition rules, and task templates instantiate the schema as paired
static, routine, and random worlds with executable N1--N5, VLN, and EQA episodes.

\subsubsection{Controlled Dynamics and Mobility Regimes}
\label{app:mobility-regime-overview}

Every relocation is attributable to an activity, resident transition, object
lifecycle event, or tidying event. Stable or rarely moved objects test whether a
method preserves valid history. Routine objects follow repeated activity chains.
Personal objects alternate between placed and carried states with owner-specific
return habits. Irregular objects use affordance-valid destinations without
temporal or actor-specific predictability (\cref{tab:mobility-regimes}). Labels
support stratified analysis but are hidden from agents.

Paired-world audits verify that personal objects do not simply track their owners
and that actor or activity identity does not predict irregular destinations beyond
affordance. For every candidate placement, physical checks record navigability,
geodesic cost, camera frustum coverage, unoccluded target fraction, and success
viewpoints, excluding impossible placements and unreachable observations.
Query times cover both the natural temporal distribution and mechanism-relevant
windows, including routine stages and intervals after personal objects are
dropped.

\subsubsection{Tasks and Evaluation Protocols}
\label{app:task-protocol-overview}

\pfdbench{} derives prediction and navigation episodes from the same target--time
queries (\cref{tab:tasks}). Prediction asks for a calibrated belief over current
regions or receptacles. A navigation episode adds a start pose, candidate
inspection viewpoints, path budget, and frozen success conditions. N1 measures
whether a Top-1 prediction becomes a useful physical decision. N2 evaluates
search over the full belief, and N3 tests recovery after an incorrect first
destination through visibility-qualified evidence. N4 additionally advances the
clock and can introduce another hidden transition. N5 is a held-out evaluation
of the preceding protocols, rather than a separate task.

The EQA suite queries existence, location, temporal order, activity association,
count, comparison, and compositional relations over causal memories. Answers are
evaluated against a normalized ontology; questions requiring future evidence are
excluded. VLN replaces the object label with a language-conditioned goal whose
referents and constraints are grounded in the same versioned history. Prediction
probes belief independently of control, navigation probes destinations and search
orders, and EQA probes the evidence retained for current and historical reasoning.

Complete temporal sequences, near-duplicate queries, and transition groups are
assigned atomically to splits, preventing future portions of a household day
from leaking into earlier decisions. Dataset cards and audit summaries expose
source mappings, authored assumptions, physical validity, candidate coverage,
and leakage tests, alongside full schemas, episode validity rules, and metric
implementations.
