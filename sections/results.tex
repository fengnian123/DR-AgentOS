\evolvingworldnav{} consistently improves the evaluated metrics across FindingDory,
GOAT-Bench, and \pfdbench{}. Across \pfdbench{} N1--N5, it improves First-Inspection SR from
45.33\% (DynaMem) to 61.32\% and Search SR from 71.94\% to 86.18\%,
showing that arrival-time prediction and en-route evidence improve the first
fully covered inspection while retained hypotheses support recovery. The gains on FindingDory
and GOAT-Bench further show that predictive memory remains effective beyond
the proposed evolving-world setting.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Core components.}
\begin{figure}[!t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{figures/core_component_ablation_grouped.pdf}
    \caption{Core component ablation; error bars show five-seed standard deviations.}
    \label{fig:module_ablation}
\end{figure}

The full agent reaches 62.17 First-Inspection SR, 85.33 Search SR, and 70.87
SPL with 3.95 inspections (\cref{fig:module_ablation}). Removing predictive
belief collapses First-Inspection SR to 12.51, while using only the latest
observation lowers Search SR by 20.91 points; both show that long-term history
guides the initial destination. Direct candidate prediction loses 13.84
First-Inspection and 15.75 Search SR points, isolating the structured
persistence--relocation belief. Top-1-only search loses 22.41 Search SR points,
and removing evidence updates loses 42.02 points, confirming that retained
alternatives and online correction drive recovery rather than exhaustive search.
