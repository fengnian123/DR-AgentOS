The groups mirror the source of each method's advantage rather than suggesting
that benchmark scores are interchangeable. \evolvingworldnav{} leads every reported
headline metric. On FindingDory, it reaches 63.22 HL-SR and 48.83 HL-SPL,
improving over the strongest competing result by 10.78 and 25.58 points. On
GOAT-Bench, its 35.43 SR, 12.98 SPL, and 19.31 Repeat-SR exceed the best baseline
by 5.79, 7.53, and 2.94 points, respectively, indicating that the same memory
interface transfers beyond explicitly evolving scenes. The advantage is most
diagnostic on \pfdbench{}: First-Go SR rises to 61.32, 15.99 points above
DynaMem, while Search SR and SPL reach 86.18 and 70.15. DynaMem remains strong
after additional search (81.00 Search SR and 66.83 SPL), but its lower First-Go
SR suggests that updating memory alone is insufficient to select the current
destination before new evidence arrives. Released navigation systems are run
through their native policies when possible; memory and prediction methods are
inserted into a shared controller as specified in \cref{sec:setup}.
Appendix tables retain the full FindingDory high-/low-level breakdown and
disaggregate GOAT category, language, and image goals.

\paragraph{Predictive navigation on \pfdbench{}.}

First-Go SR isolates whether a better current-state belief becomes a better
physical destination. Search SR and cost then test whether probability mass
beyond Top-1 is useful. Recovery SR is conditioned on an incorrect first
prediction so that easy first-attempt successes do not mask poor evidence use.
The relevant estimands are paired episode-level differences with confidence
intervals that retain the pairing.

The main table already reports First-Go SR, Search SR, and SPL. Appendix
\cref{tab:mainnav} therefore reports only the complementary measures: inspected
candidates, travel distance, and Recovery SR after an incorrect first
destination. This separation keeps the headline comparison compact while
showing whether eventual success comes from efficient evidence gathering and
belief revision rather than exhaustive search.

\paragraph{Temporal and budget dependence.}
Memory staleness, inspection budget, and mobility regime define complementary
axes of analysis. SR as a function of staleness tests robustness to observation
gaps; SR as a function of inspection budget tests the value of alternatives
beyond Top-1; and paired routine gains separate learnable temporal structure
from matched random transitions. These diagnostics require episode-level
measurements and are not inferred from aggregate SR. Appendix \cref{fig:predictive-memory}
visualizes these complementary staleness, budget, and mobility diagnostics.
In the paired control (\cref{tab:worlds}), \pfdnav{} reaches 60.85 SR
under routine evolution but 30.12 SR under matched random transitions. Its
22.36-point routine gain over Last Seen therefore localizes the main predictive
benefit to learnable temporal structure rather than implying uniform advantage
across arbitrary dynamics.


\paragraph{Generalization across simulation fidelity.}

\Cref{tab:domains} assigns one established task to each simulator rather than
pooling incompatible goal definitions: R2R-CE on MP3D, HM3D-OVON on HM3D,
PointNav on Habitat-GS, SAGE-Bench on InteriorGS, and \pfdbench{} on HSSD
\citep{krantz2020beyond,yokoyama2024hm3d,xia2026habitat,miao2026towards}.
The first group uses navigation trajectories, task-specific fine-tuning, or
reinforcement learning. The second performs zero-shot evaluation without
training a navigation policy on the target benchmark. This distinction is
important for \evolvingworldnav{}, whose controller and foundation model remain
frozen even though its world-state predictor is trained on causal histories.



All methods are evaluated with the task and success rule native to each
environment, so comparisons are made vertically within a column rather than by
pooling heterogeneous tasks. \evolvingworldnav{} leads seven of ten metrics:
it reaches 64.2/51.7 SR/SPL on HM3D-OVON, 86.2/79.8 on Habitat-GS PointNav,
42.4 SR on SAGE-Bench, and 86.6/68.2 on \pfdbench{}. Uni-LaViRA remains stronger
on R2R-CE, while NaVILA attains the best SAGE-Bench SPL. This pattern supports
broad transfer across mesh, 3DGS, and evolving environments without implying
that predictive memory supersedes task-specific navigation training in every
setting.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

We isolate the contributions of predictive history, structured state belief,
uncertainty-aware search, and online evidence updates, then test robustness to
predictor and VLM choice. Fine-grained controls are deferred to
\cref{app:fine-grained-ablation}.

\paragraph{Core components.}

The variants answer five questions: whether prediction and persistent history
matter, whether persistence--relocation factorization improves over a direct
softmax, whether the full distribution aids search, and whether observations
must update the belief.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/core_component_ablation.pdf}
    \caption{Core component ablation on embodied navigation. Error bars show five-seed standard deviations.}
    \label{fig:module_ablation}
\end{figure}

As shown in \cref{fig:module_ablation}, full \evolvingworldnav{} reaches 62.17 First-Go SR,
85.33 Search SR, and 70.87 SPL. Direct Candidate Prediction loses 15.75 Search
SR points; Top-1 Belief Only loses 22.41 points and needs 1.23 more inspections;
and disabling evidence updates produces the largest drop, to 43.31 Search SR.
Thus prediction, retained uncertainty, and the observe--update--replan loop make
distinct contributions under otherwise identical perception and control.

\paragraph{Current-state belief prediction.}
\label{sec:prediction-results}

Before coupling prediction with navigation, we first isolate whether long-term
memory can recover the current object state from stale observations.
\Cref{tab:predictors} evaluates two complementary settings.
The temporal split contains changed-only queries and therefore removes trivial
cases in which the last-seen state remains correct, while the LOSO split uses
change-balanced queries to additionally evaluate transfer to held-out scenes.
The absolute Top-1 values across the two settings are thus not directly
comparable.

On temporal changed-only queries, Ours is best across all three metrics,
reaching $38.24\pm1.42$\% Top-1, $0.5971\pm0.0291$ MRR, and
$1.4925\pm0.0867$ NLL. Relative to the strongest shared semantic prior, this is
a 2.22-point Top-1 gain while also reducing NLL from 1.5766 to 1.4925. The
consistent improvement in both argmax accuracy and distributional quality is
important because the downstream agent uses the full location belief to order
physical inspections.

Under LOSO evaluation, Ours again achieves the best result across all three
metrics, reaching $61.18\pm4.53$\% Top-1, $0.7523\pm0.0176$ MRR, and
$0.9778\pm0.0228$ NLL.
Compared with the strongest shared semantic prior, Ours improves
Top-1 by 1.71 percentage points and reduces NLL from 1.0448 to 0.9778.
These results suggest that structured long-term memory contributes information
beyond shared semantic regularities and improves the transfer of current-state
beliefs to unseen scenes.



\Cref{fig:belief-calibration} complements the ranking metrics with probability
quality. The structured prior follows empirical accuracy more closely than the
direct Transformer, while evidence updating maintains this reliability and
concentrates more predictions in the high-confidence bins. Thus, the update
mechanism improves an action-facing belief rather than merely sharpening an
uncalibrated score, which is essential when confidence determines the next
inspection target.

Because this table isolates prediction from embodied execution, it complements
rather than duplicates the navigation ablation in \cref{fig:module_ablation}.
Together, the two experiments connect better calibrated current-state belief to
more efficient search. Full predictor controls and diagnostics are reported in
\cref{app:prediction-evidence}.

\paragraph{Foundation-model robustness.}

We pair the last-seen baseline and \evolvingworldnav{} on identical episodes,
tools, prompts, budgets, and low-level control while varying only the frozen
VLM. The study spans GPT-4o, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6-Luna, and open-weight
Qwen2.5-VL-3B/32B \citep{hurst2024gpt4o,openai2026gpt55,
openai2026gpt56luna,bai2025qwen25vl,qiao2025navbench}. Across all five
controllers, predictive memory improves First-Go SR by 22.30 points, Search SR
by 20.90 points, and SPL by 0.2525 on average; every individual Search-SR gain
is positive (14.91--25.93 points). This within-controller consistency, together
with the VLM-independent prediction results in \cref{tab:predictors}, attributes
the gain to world-state belief and evidence updating rather than controller
scale or provider. The full paired matrix is in Appendix
\cref{tab:foundation_models}.
