\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

The evaluation separates six questions: performance across embodied benchmarks,
predictive navigation on \pfdbench{}, visual-domain generalization, the role of
individual components, controller dependence, and indoor/outdoor object search.
\emph{Draft data status:} \na{} denotes an unavailable measurement. The real-world
table contains explicitly marked numerical placeholders; its associated analysis
is provisional and must be recomputed from robot outcomes before submission.

\subsection{Predictive Navigation on P4D-Bench}

First-Go SR isolates whether a better current-state belief becomes a better
physical destination. Search SR and cost then test whether probability mass
beyond Top-1 is useful. Recovery SR is conditioned on an incorrect first
prediction so that easy first-attempt successes do not mask poor evidence use.
The relevant estimands are paired episode-level differences with confidence
intervals that retain the pairing.

\paragraph{Qualitative case study.}
\Cref{fig:p4d_case_study} traces one routine-world episode from stale memory to
physical verification. The target bowl was last observed in a cabinet
$14.77$ hours before the query, but a dinner-cleanup transition relocates it to
the kitchen sink. The predictive belief ranks the sink first, allowing
\pfdnav{} to verify the target in one inspection; the comparison routes expose
how an obsolete last-seen state or a geometry-only ordering increases search
cost.

\paragraph{Temporal and budget dependence.}
Memory staleness, inspection budget, and mobility regime define complementary
axes of analysis. SR as a function of staleness tests robustness to observation
gaps; SR as a function of inspection budget tests the value of alternatives
beyond Top-1; and paired routine gains separate learnable temporal structure
from matched random transitions. These diagnostics require episode-level
measurements and are not inferred from aggregate SR.

\paragraph{Indoor search.}
In the provisional comparison, the \dragentos{} slot reaches 16/32 successes
(50.00\%), compared with 14/32 for Time Frequency and 13/32 for Last Seen +
Search. The corresponding differences are $+6.25$ and $+9.38$ percentage points,
computed from counts before rounding. Retrieval + Reasoning occupies the
highest-success slot at 17/32 (53.12\%). The \dragentos{} slot has a mean elapsed
time of 74.56 seconds, compared with 96.53 seconds for Retrieval + Reasoning,
but travels farther (20.23 versus 18.29 meters). Thus, the numerical example
illustrates a success--time tradeoff, rather than uniform dominance in success
and travel cost.

\paragraph{Outdoor search.}
The outdoor placeholders give 12/32 successes (37.50\%) for the \dragentos{}
slot and 13/32 (40.62\%) for each comparison method. Its mean elapsed time is
106.37 seconds, compared with 109.13 seconds for Time Frequency and 128.51
seconds for Retrieval + Reasoning. Its travel distance is lower than Time
Frequency's (42.22 versus 43.87 meters) but higher than Retrieval + Reasoning's
(38.92 meters). A shorter attempt at lower SR does not establish more efficient
successful search: early failures can reduce both elapsed time and distance.

\paragraph{Aggregate contrast and uncertainty.}
The primary contrast is the equally weighted mean of the two within-environment
SR differences between \dragentos{} and Time Frequency. In the placeholder
counts, this is $(6.25-3.125)/2=1.5625$ percentage points, corresponding to
28/64 versus 27/64 successes. Pooling the equal-sized environments gives the
same result but obscures the reversal in the within-environment comparison.
These descriptive differences do not establish a statistically reliable gain.
Physical paired uncertainty requires the actual block outcomes and object/day
identities; quantiles across synthetic studies cannot supply that uncertainty.

\paragraph{Evidence use and failure analysis.}
Aggregate SR and attempt costs do not determine whether a failure arose from
an incorrect belief, poor visibility, missed detection, or navigation error.
Likewise, they do not establish the contribution of posterior updating or
continuous-time prediction. First-inspection confirmation, recovery conditional
on a wrong initial choice, and the history/action ablations therefore require
separate run-level evidence. A recovery case links the causal history and initial
belief to an executed inspection, its sensor evidence, the posterior, and the
next action. No physical case trace or recovery rate is inferred from the
placeholder table. \Cref{sec:real-world-setup} defines the corresponding
measurement protocol.
