\paragraph{Temporal and budget dependence.}
Memory staleness, inspection budget, and mobility regime define complementary
axes of analysis. SR as a function of staleness tests robustness to observation
gaps; SR as a function of inspection budget tests the value of alternatives
beyond Top-1; and paired routine gains separate learnable temporal structure
from matched random transitions. These diagnostics require episode-level
measurements and are not inferred from aggregate SR.

\paragraph{Predictive memory improves budgeted real-world search.}
We compare four methods on the primary LYNX M20 platform using 64 matched
search blocks per method, equally divided between indoor and outdoor
environments. Each run allows at most three inspection actions and 360 seconds,
including inference, navigation, and observation.

For this evaluation, First-Go SR requires successful navigation and autonomous
target confirmation during the first inspection; it does not count a correct
location prediction alone as success. Search SR measures confirmation within
the complete episode budget. Episodes that terminate before the first
inspection remain failures in both metrics.

As shown in \cref{tab:real-main}, Last Seen + Search succeeds in 17 of 64
episodes (26.6\%). Time Frequency improves this to 20 of 64 (31.3\%), while
Retrieval + Reasoning succeeds in 24 of 64 (37.5\%). \dragentos{} completes
31 of 64 episodes (48.4\%), improving over Retrieval + Reasoning by
10.9 percentage points.

First-Go SR increases from 26.6\% for Retrieval + Reasoning to 34.4\% for
\dragentos{}. Thus, part of the overall improvement is already present at
the first inspection, while subsequent search accounts for additional
successful episodes. Nevertheless, more than half of the \dragentos{}
episodes remain unsuccessful under the fixed budget, leaving substantial
room for improvement in end-to-end reliability.

Mean all-attempt travel distance decreases from 50.3\,m for Retrieval +
Reasoning to 43.8\,m for \dragentos{}, a reduction of 12.9\%. Mean elapsed
time decreases from 281\,s to 248\,s, and the number of completed inspections
decreases from 2.03 to 1.84. Because early termination can also shorten failed
runs, these cost measures are interpreted jointly with Search SR rather than
as standalone evidence of search efficiency.

\paragraph{Indoor and outdoor search exhibit different difficulty.}
\Cref{tab:real-domain} separates the M20 results by environment.
Indoors, \dragentos{} succeeds in 17 of 32 episodes (53.1\%), compared with
13 of 32 (40.6\%) for Retrieval + Reasoning. Outdoors, it succeeds in
14 of 32 episodes (43.8\%), compared with 11 of 32 (34.4\%) for the same

The improvement is therefore present in both settings, but the outdoor
evaluation remains more difficult in this configuration. Outdoor runs also
incur longer travel distances: for \dragentos{}, mean distance is 65.2\,m

\paragraph{The benefit depends on the temporal structure of the change.}
\Cref{tab:real-condition} reports Search SR under the four temporal
conditions, with 16 episodes per condition and method.

When the target remains unchanged, Last Seen + Search achieves the highest
success rate, 68.8\%, compared with 62.5\% for \dragentos{}. This condition
exposes an important trade-off: projecting historical observations forward
does not necessarily improve search when the most recent observation is
already correct.

Under routine-consistent changes, Time Frequency reaches 50.0\% and
outperforms Retrieval + Reasoning at 43.8\%. \dragentos{} reaches 68.8\%,
showing its largest advantage when the history contains recurring changes
that are informative about the current state.

Performance decreases for all methods as these regularities weaken or
break. \dragentos{} achieves 37.5\% on weak-routine episodes and 25.0\% on
broken-routine episodes. Retrieval + Reasoning achieves 25.0\% and 18.8\%,
respectively. In particular, the broken-routine advantage corresponds to
only one additional success among 16 episodes and should not be interpreted
as strong evidence of reliable recovery under arbitrary changes.

Overall, the condition breakdown distinguishes exploiting informative
temporal structure from solving unpredictable relocations. Predictive
memory is most useful when historical changes contain a usable signal,
while routine violations remain challenging under a limited search budget.

\paragraph{Online correction recovers some unsuccessful first inspections.}
Recovery SR is evaluated over episodes that complete a first inspection
without confirming the target. Episodes terminated before any inspection
are excluded from this conditional metric but remain failures in the
overall evaluation.

For \dragentos{}, 22 episodes succeed at the first inspection and five
terminate before any inspection. Of the remaining 37 episodes, nine
eventually succeed, yielding a Recovery SR of 24.3\%. The corresponding
counts are 6/47 for Last Seen + Search, 6/43 for Time Frequency, and 7/41
for Retrieval + Reasoning.

These results show that later observations and additional search can
contribute to task completion, but recovery remains limited: most
unsuccessful first inspections are not resolved within the remaining
budget. The system therefore cannot rely on repeated search to compensate
for an inaccurate initial belief.

The conditional recovery sets differ across methods, so these rates are
descriptive rather than a controlled estimate of the effect of Bayesian
updating. Isolating that effect requires the matched no-update ablation,
with the initial belief and execution conditions held fixed. Moreover,
failure to observe a target is treated as uncertain negative evidence,
rather than proof that the target is absent.

\paragraph{Cross-platform deployment preserves the direction of the gain.}
We evaluate Last Seen + Search and \dragentos{} on a preselected
16-block transfer subset, balanced across indoor and outdoor environments
and the four temporal conditions. The corresponding M20 runs are drawn
from the main evaluation rather than counted as additional trials.
X30 and Lite3 each execute the same subset.

As shown in \cref{tab:real-transfer}, \dragentos{} succeeds in 8 of 16
episodes on M20 and 7 of 16 on both X30 and Lite3. Last Seen + Search
succeeds in 4, 4, and 5 episodes, respectively. The direction of the
improvement is consistent across the three platforms, while the magnitude
varies.

The memory representation, predictor, belief-update rule, and high-level
search policy are unchanged across platforms. These results are compatible
with cross-platform reuse of the predictive-search stack, but the small
transfer set does not establish equivalent performance across robots.
Each additional success changes the reported rate by 6.25 percentage
points, and absolute differences between platforms should therefore be
interpreted cautiously.

Taken together, the evaluation characterizes predictive memory as a useful
but imperfect search prior. Its strongest benefit appears under recurring
changes, where historical observations can inform the current state.
Online observations allow some incorrect or unconfirmed hypotheses to be
revised, but weak regularity, routine violations, and the finite execution
budget continue to limit overall success.
